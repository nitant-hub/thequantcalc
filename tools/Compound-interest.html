Understanding Compound Interest

Compound interest is often referred to as the "eighth wonder of the world," and for good reason. Unlike simple interest, which only pays returns on your original principal, compound interest allows you to earn returns on both your initial investment and the accumulated interest from previous periods.

By continually reinvesting your earnings, your wealth undergoes exponential growth over time. Our Compound Interest Calculator makes it easy to visualize this growth trajectory based on your initial deposit, expected return rate, time horizon, and compounding frequency.

How Compounding Works

When you invest money, the growth follows a powerful cycle:

  • Initial Phase: In the first period, your interest is calculated strictly on the money you originally deposited.
  • Reinvestment Phase: In the next period, your new balance (Principal + Period 1 Interest) is used as the base for the next calculation.
  • The Snowball Effect: Because your balance gets larger every cycle, the absolute amount of interest you earn also increases, creating an upward curve of wealth generation.

The Compound Interest Formula

The math driving our calculator uses the standard algebraic formula for compounding:

A = P × (1 + r/n)nt

Here is what each variable represents:

  • A (Amount): The estimated final future value of your investment.
  • P (Principal): Your starting investment balance.
  • r (Annual Interest Rate): The yearly rate expressed as a decimal (e.g., 8% becomes 0.08).
  • n (Compounding Frequency): How many times per year the interest is calculated and added to the balance.
  • t (Time Period): The number of years the money is invested.

How Compounding Frequency Impacts Your Returns

The variable n in the formula represents how often your interest is compounded. Generally, the more frequently compounding occurs, the higher your final balance will be, assuming the interest rate remains identical.

  • Annually: Interest is calculated and added once per year (n=1).
  • Quarterly: Interest is calculated four times per year (n=4).
  • Monthly: Interest is calculated twelve times per year (n=12). This is common for many savings accounts and credit cards.
  • Daily: Interest is calculated every single day (n=365), maximizing the compounding effect.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is compound interest?

Compound interest is the interest on a deposit calculated based on both the initial principal and the accumulated interest from previous periods. It essentially means earning interest on your interest.

How does compounding frequency affect my returns?

The more frequently interest is compounded (e.g., daily vs. annually), the more money you will earn, because the interest is added to your principal balance sooner, allowing it to generate its own interest faster.

What is the formula for compound interest?

The standard formula is A = P × (1 + r/n)^(nt), where A is the final amount, P is the principal, r is the annual interest rate, n is the number of compounding periods per year, and t is the time in years.
