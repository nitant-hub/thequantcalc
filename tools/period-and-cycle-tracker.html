How to use this period tracker

  1. Choose the first day of your most recent period.
  2. Enter your usual cycle length, counted from the first day of one period to the first day of the next.
  3. Enter your typical period duration and, if known, your estimated luteal-phase length.
  4. Select Calculate to see your estimated next period, ovulation date, fertile window and cycle day.

The calculator uses the information you enter for this calculation. If you are unsure of your usual cycle length, predictions may be less useful; tracking several cycles can help you understand your own pattern.

Important: Menstrual cycles and ovulation timing can vary from month to month. This tool is for general educational information only. It cannot diagnose a condition, confirm ovulation, determine whether you are pregnant, or reliably identify days when pregnancy is impossible.

How are the dates estimated?

Next period

The estimated next period is calculated by adding your selected cycle length to the first day of your last period. For example, a 28-day cycle estimate places the next period about 28 days after the previous period began. Real cycles do not always follow an exact calendar pattern.

Ovulation date

Ovulation is estimated by counting backward from the predicted next period using the luteal-phase length you enter. The calculation is an approximation: ovulation does not always occur on the same cycle day, and a calendar estimate cannot confirm that ovulation happened.

Fertile window

The fertile window is commonly described as the five days before ovulation and the day of ovulation. This estimate can be wrong when ovulation timing changes. Do not rely on a period-tracking calendar as a method of contraception.

Understanding the menstrual cycle

  • Menstrual phase: the days when menstrual bleeding occurs.
  • Follicular phase: begins on the first day of the period and continues until ovulation; its length can vary.
  • Ovulation: an ovary releases an egg. The exact date can vary, even in people whose cycles usually seem regular.
  • Luteal phase: the phase after ovulation and before the next period. Its duration also varies.

These phase descriptions overlap in timing—for example, the follicular phase begins on cycle day 1, during menstruation. The timeline shown by this tool is simplified to make the estimate easier to read, not to represent every biological change.

Menstrual cycle phases at a glance

The table below summarizes the main phases of a menstrual cycle. Actual timing varies between people and from one cycle to another.

Phase Typical timing What happens
Menstrual Cycle begins on day 1 The uterine lining sheds, causing menstrual bleeding.
Follicular Day 1 until ovulation Follicles develop, and the uterine lining begins to build up.
Ovulation Varies by cycle An ovary releases an egg.
Luteal After ovulation until the next period Hormonal changes prepare the uterine lining for a possible pregnancy.

These phases can overlap in timing: the follicular phase begins on the first day of menstruation. This table is a general reference, not a prediction of your hormone levels.

When calendar estimates may be less accurate

Predictions may be less reliable during adolescence, around perimenopause, after pregnancy, while breastfeeding, after stopping hormonal contraception, or when cycles are irregular. Stress, illness, travel, changes in weight and some health conditions can also affect cycle timing.

Consider speaking with a healthcare professional if your periods become persistently irregular, you miss periods and pregnancy is possible, you have severe pain, bleeding between periods, or bleeding that is unusually heavy for you. Seek urgent care for severe symptoms such as fainting or intense, sudden pelvic pain.

Frequently asked questions

How does a period tracker estimate my next period?

It adds your usual cycle length to the first day of your last period. This is an estimate; the next period may arrive earlier or later.

How is ovulation estimated?

This tracker counts backward from the estimated next period using the luteal-phase length you enter. Actual ovulation timing can vary, so this does not confirm ovulation.

What is the fertile window?

It is commonly described as the five days before ovulation and the day of ovulation. Calendar estimates cannot reliably confirm fertile days or prevent pregnancy.

Can I use this tracker as birth control?

No. Calendar predictions are not reliable contraception and should not be used to avoid pregnancy.

What if my cycles are irregular?

Predictions may be less accurate when cycle lengths vary. Tracking several cycles and discussing significant changes with a healthcare professional may help.

What counts as the first day of a period?

Cycle day 1 is generally the first day of menstrual bleeding, rather than the day before bleeding starts.

Does this tracker save my cycle information?

This page's calculator code processes the entries in your browser and does not send them to a server or store them. Your browser, extensions or the wider website may have their own behavior, so avoid entering information on a device you do not trust.

When should I seek medical advice?

Consider medical advice for a missed period with possible pregnancy, very heavy bleeding, severe pain, bleeding between periods, or a major persistent change in your cycle. Seek urgent help for severe or sudden symptoms.
