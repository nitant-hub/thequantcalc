What the Period and Cycle Tracker Does

This free period and cycle tracker estimates the start of your next menstrual period, the likely day of ovulation, and the approximate fertile window. It uses the first day of your last menstrual period (LMP) together with your personal average cycle length. All calculations run locally in your browser; nothing you enter is uploaded or stored by TheQuantCalc.

The tool is designed for people who want a clear, evidence-based estimate of upcoming cycle dates for planning, awareness, or simply understanding their own pattern. It is not a medical device and is not a substitute for professional advice or reliable contraception.

Formulas and How the Estimates Are Made

The core relationship is straightforward:

Next period start = First day of last period + Average cycle length (in days)

Ovulation is estimated by working backwards from the expected next period:

Estimated ovulation day ≈ Cycle length − Luteal phase length
(default luteal phase = 14 days)

The fertile window is approximated as the five days before the estimated ovulation day plus the day of ovulation itself.

Worked Example

Suppose the first day of your last period was 1 March and your average cycle length is 28 days, with a luteal phase of 14 days and a typical period lasting 5 days.

  • Next period start = 1 March + 28 days = 29 March
  • Estimated ovulation day = day 14 → 14 March
  • Approximate fertile window = 9 March to 15 March
  • Expected period duration = 29 March to 2 April

Normal Ranges and What Variation Means

A typical adult cycle length is 21–35 days. Periods commonly last 3–7 days. Occasional variation of a few days is common and can be caused by stress, travel, illness, weight change, intense exercise or disrupted sleep. Tracking at least three consecutive cycles gives a more useful personal average.

Assumptions and Limitations

  • Assumes a relatively regular cycle and a luteal phase close to 14 days.
  • Does not account for anovulatory cycles, PCOS, perimenopause, recent pregnancy, breastfeeding or hormonal contraception.
  • Calendar estimates alone are not reliable for contraception.
  • Results are estimates only and cannot replace medical advice.

Practical Uses

  • Planning travel, events or physical activity around expected bleeding days
  • Increasing awareness of the approximate fertile window
  • Providing a personal average cycle length for discussions with a clinician
  • Noticing larger pattern changes that may warrant professional attention

Frequently Asked Questions

**Summary:** ▶ 1. How is the next period date calculated?

The next period start date is calculated by adding your average cycle length (in days) to the first day of your last menstrual period (LMP). For example, if your last period began on 1 March and your cycle length is 28 days, the next period is expected on 29 March.

**Summary:** ▶ 2. What is a normal menstrual cycle length?

A normal cycle length is usually between 21 and 35 days, measured from the first day of one period to the first day of the next. The commonly cited average is 28 days, but only a minority of people have an exactly 28-day cycle. Consistency matters more than matching the textbook number.

**Summary:** ▶ 3. How is ovulation estimated?

Ovulation is estimated by subtracting a typical luteal-phase length of 14 days from the cycle length. On a 28-day cycle this places ovulation around day 14. The fertile window is then approximated as the five days before ovulation plus the day of ovulation itself, because sperm can survive several days.

**Summary:** ▶ 4. Can this calculator be used as contraception?

No. Calendar-based predictions have a relatively high typical-use failure rate and are not reliable enough to prevent pregnancy on their own. Always use a proven method of contraception if pregnancy prevention is your goal, and consult a healthcare professional for personal advice.

**Summary:** ▶ 5. Why do my cycles vary from month to month?

Stress, travel, illness, significant weight change, intense exercise, sleep disruption and hormonal fluctuations can all shift cycle length. Variation of up to about seven days is common. Larger or persistent changes should be discussed with a doctor.

**Summary:** ▶ 6. How many cycles should I track for a reliable average?

Track at least three consecutive cycles for a useful average. Six or more cycles give a more stable personal average and better reveal your usual range of variation.

**Summary:** ▶ 7. What is the difference between cycle length and period duration?

Cycle length is the number of days from the first day of one period to the first day of the next period. Period duration is simply how many days the bleeding lasts (typically 3–7 days). They are separate measurements.

**Summary:** ▶ 8. Does the calculator store my data?

No. All calculations run entirely in your browser. The dates and numbers you enter are not sent to any server and are not stored by TheQuantCalc.
