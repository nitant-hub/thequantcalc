IOM & ACOG Pregnancy Weight Gain Guidelines

In 2009, the Institute of Medicine (now the National Academy of Medicine), endorsed by the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG), established gestational weight gain guidelines categorized by pre-pregnancy BMI.

Pre-pregnancy Category BMI (kg/m²) Singleton Total Gain Weekly Gain (Trimesters 2 & 3) Twin Total Gain
Underweight < 18.5 28–40 lbs (12.5–18.0 kg) ~1.0 lb/wk (0.5 kg/wk) Insufficient data
Normal Weight 18.5–24.9 25–35 lbs (11.5–16.0 kg) ~1.0 lb/wk (0.4 kg/wk) 37–54 lbs (16.8–24.5 kg)
Overweight 25.0–29.9 15–25 lbs (7.0–11.5 kg) ~0.6 lb/wk (0.3 kg/wk) 31–50 lbs (14.1–22.7 kg)
Obese ≥ 30.0 11–20 lbs (5.0–9.0 kg) ~0.5 lb/wk (0.2 kg/wk) 25–42 lbs (11.3–19.1 kg)

Source: Institute of Medicine (IOM) / American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) Committee Opinion No. 548.

Where does the pregnancy weight go?

A common misconception is that all pregnancy weight gain represents maternal body fat. In reality, a full-term pregnancy distributes weight across multiple physiological components:

  • Baby: 7 to 8 lbs (3.0 to 3.6 kg)
  • Placenta: 1.5 lbs (0.7 kg)
  • Amniotic fluid: 2 lbs (0.9 kg)
  • Uterine growth: 2 lbs (0.9 kg)
  • Maternal breast tissue: 2 lbs (0.9 kg)
  • Increased blood volume: 3 to 4 lbs (1.4 to 1.8 kg)
  • Maternal body fluids: 3 to 4 lbs (1.4 to 1.8 kg)
  • Maternal fat and nutrient stores: 6 to 8 lbs (2.7 to 3.6 kg)

Trimester-by-trimester weight gain patterns

First trimester (Weeks 1 to 13)

During the first 13 weeks, the embryo and placenta are microscopic or small. Normal weight gain is modest: typically between 1.0 and 4.5 lbs (0.5 to 2.0 kg) total. Due to morning sickness and nausea, some individuals gain very little or temporarily lose slight weight, which is usually resolved in the second trimester.

Second & third trimesters (Weeks 14 to 40)

Fetal bone ossification, organ maturation, fat accumulation, and maternal fluid expansion accelerate. Weight gain typically becomes regular, progressing at approximately 0.5 to 1.0 lb per week depending on starting BMI.

Frequently asked questions

How is recommended pregnancy weight gain calculated?

Clinical guidelines calculate your baseline Body Mass Index (BMI = weight in kg / height in meters squared). Targets are set using IOM standards based on whether your starting BMI is categorized as underweight, normal, overweight, or obese.

How much weight should I gain during the first trimester?

Clinical recommendations target an overall gain of 1.0 to 4.5 lbs (0.5 to 2.0 kg) throughout the entire first trimester (weeks 1 to 13).

How much weight should I gain per week in the second and third trimesters?

Normal-weight individuals typically aim for ~1.0 lb (0.4–0.5 kg) per week. Overweight individuals aim for ~0.6 lb (0.3 kg) per week, and obese individuals aim for ~0.5 lb (0.2 kg) per week.

What if I am carrying twins?

Twin pregnancies have higher caloric requirements. Normal-weight women expecting twins are advised to gain 37–54 lbs (16.8–24.5 kg) to support dual fetal growth.

Is it safe to lose weight while pregnant?

No. Weight loss diets during pregnancy risk depriving the fetus of essential macronutrients, vitamins, and minerals. Consult your maternal healthcare provider if you have concerns about your weight.
