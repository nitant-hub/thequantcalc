How to use the pregnancy calculator

  1. Choose Last period date if you know the first day of your last menstrual period, or choose Known due date if a healthcare professional has already given you an estimated due date.
  2. If using the last-period method, enter your usual cycle length. The default is 28 days.
  3. Select Calculate pregnancy timeline to view your estimated gestational age, due date, trimester, progress and countdown.
  4. Use the trimester tracker as a general calendar guide, not as a clinical assessment of pregnancy development.

Important health note: This calculator cannot confirm pregnancy, determine whether a pregnancy is progressing normally, diagnose a condition or assess the health of a baby. Contact a qualified healthcare professional for pregnancy confirmation, dating and prenatal care. Seek urgent care for severe or worrying symptoms.

How is the estimated due date calculated?

Pregnancy is commonly dated as approximately 40 weeks (280 days) from the first day of the last menstrual period. This is a dating convention, not a statement that conception occurred on that day. In a typical 28-day cycle, ovulation and conception often occur around two weeks after the period begins, but timing varies.

When you select the last-period method, this calculator adds 280 days to the date you enter and adjusts the estimate by the difference between your selected cycle length and 28 days. This simple adjustment may be unsuitable for irregular cycles or uncertain dates. A clinician may revise the estimated due date using your history and ultrasound findings.

If you choose the known due-date method, the calculator uses the date you provide to estimate the current gestational age. Use the date from your clinician if available.

What is gestational age?

Gestational age is usually expressed in completed weeks and days counted from the first day of the last menstrual period. For example, 10 weeks and 3 days means ten complete weeks and three additional days have elapsed according to that dating method. It does not mean that conception happened ten weeks ago.

The estimate shown here is calculated from calendar dates. It may differ from the gestational age assigned by your healthcare professional, particularly when periods are irregular, the last-period date is unknown or ultrasound measurements suggest a different date.

Understanding the trimester countdown

Pregnancy is commonly discussed in three trimesters. Boundaries are described slightly differently across medical resources, so this tool uses a simplified convention for a clear timeline: first trimester through week 13, second trimester from week 14 through week 27, and third trimester from week 28 onward.

How should I interpret the countdown?

The countdown is the number of calendar days between today and the estimated due date. It is not a prediction of the exact day labour will begin. A pregnancy may continue beyond the estimated due date or end before it, and your care team can advise you about appropriate monitoring and next steps.

The progress percentage is a simple visual comparison against a 280-day dating period. It is capped at 100% for display and is not a measurement of fetal growth, health or readiness for birth.

When is this calculator less reliable?

Calendar calculations may be less reliable if your periods are irregular, you do not remember the first day of your last period, you recently stopped hormonal contraception, you have recently been pregnant or given birth, or you are unsure of the date used by your clinician. Cycle length alone cannot establish the exact date of ovulation or conception.

If you think you may be pregnant, consider an appropriate pregnancy test and arrange care with a qualified healthcare professional. Seek prompt medical advice for concerns such as bleeding or persistent pain, and urgent care for severe pain, heavy bleeding, fainting or other severe symptoms.

Frequently asked questions

How is my estimated due date calculated?

With the last-period method, the estimate is 280 days (40 weeks) from the first day of your last menstrual period, adjusted here for your selected cycle length. A clinician may use other information to confirm or revise the date.

How many weeks pregnant am I?

Gestational age is commonly counted from the first day of the last menstrual period. This calculator estimates completed weeks and extra days from the date you enter or from a known due date.

When does each trimester begin?

This tool uses weeks 1–13 for the first trimester, weeks 14–27 for the second, and week 28 onward for the third as a simplified guide. Your clinician may describe boundaries differently.

Can this calculator confirm pregnancy?

No. It estimates dates only and cannot confirm pregnancy, assess fetal health or replace a pregnancy test or clinical assessment.

Is the estimated due date exact?

No. A due date is an estimate, and babies may be born before or after it. Your healthcare professional may adjust dating based on clinical information and ultrasound.

What if I do not know my last period date?

If you know a due date supplied by a healthcare professional, select the known due-date option. If you are unsure of your dates, ask a healthcare professional about appropriate pregnancy dating.

Does this calculator save my pregnancy information?

The calculator runs its calculations in your browser and does not include code to send or store the entered dates. Your browser, extensions or the wider website may have their own behavior, so avoid entering private information on a device you do not trust.

When should I contact a healthcare professional?

Arrange prenatal care to confirm dating and discuss your health. Seek urgent medical advice for severe pain, heavy bleeding, fainting or other severe or worrying symptoms during pregnancy.
