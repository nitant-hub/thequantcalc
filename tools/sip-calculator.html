What Is a SIP and Why Does the Math Matter?

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) lets you invest a fixed amount every month into a mutual fund instead of trying to time the market with a large lump sum. Most investors know that "starting early helps," but few can say by how much. That is exactly the gap this SIP calculator fills.

Enter your monthly amount, the annual return you expect, and how many years you plan to stay invested. The calculator compounds every single monthly instalment for the exact number of months it remains in the market, then shows your total invested amount, estimated returns, and final maturity value.

The Formula Behind the SIP Calculator

SIP maturity value is the future value of a series of equal payments made at the start of each month (an annuity due):

FV = P × [ ((1 + i)n − 1) / i ] × (1 + i)
P = monthly investment  |  i = annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100  |  n = years × 12

Each instalment earns returns for a different length of time. Your first payment compounds for all n months, while your last compounds for just one. That is why the early instalments do most of the heavy lifting.

How to Get More Out of Your SIP

  • Start early: Ten extra years of compounding can matter more than doubling your monthly amount.
  • Stay consistent: Pausing a SIP during market dips means missing the cheaper units that boost long-term returns.
  • Be realistic with returns: Use a conservative rate when planning. Equity returns vary widely from year to year.
  • Increase gradually: Raising your SIP when your income grows can speed up your goal significantly.

Disclaimer: This calculator provides estimates based on a constant annual return. Mutual fund investments are subject to market risks and returns are not guaranteed. Results do not account for exit loads, expense ratios or taxes. This tool is for educational purposes and is not financial advice.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

1. What is a SIP and how does a SIP calculator work?

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) invests a fixed amount at regular monthly intervals. A SIP calculator estimates the maturity value by compounding each monthly instalment at the expected rate of return for the remaining months of your investment period.

2. Are SIP returns guaranteed?

No. Mutual fund returns are market-linked and not guaranteed. The calculator shows an estimate based on the constant annual return you enter. Actual returns will vary.

3. Which formula does this SIP calculator use?

It uses the future value of an annuity due: FV = P × [((1 + i)^n − 1) / i] × (1 + i), where P is the monthly investment, i is the monthly rate (annual rate / 12 / 100) and n is the total number of months.
