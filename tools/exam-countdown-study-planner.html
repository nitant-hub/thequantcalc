Turn Your Exam Dates Into a Daily Study Timetable

When several exams are coming, the hardest part is not studying; it is deciding what to study today. Most students either split time equally across subjects or study whatever feels most urgent, and both approaches waste hours. The Exam Countdown & Study Hours Allocator solves this with a transparent rule. You enter each exam date, how difficult the subject is and how confident you feel now, plus the hours you can study on weekdays and weekends. The calculator then shows a live countdown for every exam and a day-by-day timetable that splits your hours across subjects.

It runs in your browser, needs no account and saves your inputs on your device so the countdown is waiting when you return.

How the Hours Are Allocated

Each study day, the calculator divides that day’s available time among the subjects whose exams are still ahead. A subject receives a larger share when it is harder, when you are less confident in it and when its exam is closer.

Priority weight = Difficulty × (6 − Confidence)
Urgency boost = 1 + 7 ÷ (days left until that exam)
Daily score = Priority weight × Urgency boost
Subject’s share of the day = Daily score ÷ Sum of all active scores
  1. Weight. Difficulty and confidence are each rated 1 to 5. A hard subject (4) you feel weak in (2) gets 4 × 4 = 16. An easy subject (2) you feel good about (4) gets 2 × 2 = 4.
  2. Urgency. The boost grows as an exam approaches: ×1.23 with 30 days left, ×1.5 with 14 days left, ×2 with 7 days left and ×8 on the last day. This pushes the nearest exam up the queue without ignoring later ones.
  3. Daily split. Each day’s time is divided in proportion to the scores and rounded to 5-minute units using the largest-remainder method, so the subjects always add up exactly to your available hours.
  4. Exam day. A subject stops receiving time after the day before its exam, and its hours flow to the remaining subjects.

The constant 7 is a design choice, not a law of learning. It means that one week before an exam, urgency doubles a subject’s score. You can see the effect immediately by changing the inputs.

How to Use the Calculator

  1. Set the start date (today by default), your weekday and weekend study hours and an optional weekly rest day.
  2. Add each subject with its exam date and start time, difficulty and confidence. Rate honestly: an overconfident rating reduces the time a weak subject receives.
  3. Optionally enter the total hours you think a subject needs. The tool then tells you whether your available time covers it.
  4. Read Today’s study plan, then open the weekly and daily tables. Download the CSV to import the schedule into a spreadsheet or calendar.

Step-by-Step Worked Examples

Example 1: Three Exams, 20 Study Days

Start date Tuesday 1 December 2026, with 4 hours on weekdays, 6 hours on weekends and no rest day. Maths is on 15 December (difficulty 4, confidence 2), Physics on 18 December (3, 3) and English on 21 December (2, 4).

  1. Weights: Maths 4 × 4 = 16; Physics 3 × 3 = 9; English 2 × 2 = 4.
  2. Available time: 1 to 20 December has 14 weekdays and 6 weekend days: 14 × 4 + 6 × 6 = 92 hours over 20 study days.
  3. Day 1 scores (1 December): days left are 14, 17 and 20, so urgency is 1.5, 1.412 and 1.35. Scores: Maths 24.0, Physics 12.71, English 5.4; the total is 42.11.
  4. Day 1 split of 4 hours (48 five-minute units): exact shares are 27.4, 14.5 and 6.2 units. After rounding with the largest remainder: Maths 2h 15m, Physics 1h 15m, English 30m.
  5. Day 14 (14 December, the day before Maths): Maths receives 3h 10m, Physics 40m and English 10m. On 15 December, Maths drops out and the 4 hours go to Physics (3h 05m) and English (55m).

Example 2: Checking Whether Your Time Is Enough

In the same plan, suppose you estimate that Physics needs 30 hours and English needs 20 hours. The calculator compares each target with the planned time:

  1. Physics: 30h − 27h 10m = a shortfall of 2h 50m, shown as “Short by 2h 50m”.
  2. English: 24h 55m − 20h = 4h 55m of spare time, shown as “On track (+4h 55m)”.
  3. What to do: add study hours, convert a rest day into a study day, or lower the Physics target. Extra hours are shared across all subjects by priority, so add a little more than the gap. The warning disappears once every target is covered.

Making the Plan Work in Real Life

  • Use focus blocks. The plan converts each subject’s time into 25-minute blocks. Short, focused blocks with a five-minute break are a popular way to stay concentrated.
  • Revise rather than only read. Practice questions and past papers show what you do not know. Spend the last days before an exam on them.
  • Protect rest. A weekly rest day and enough sleep help memory and focus. A plan that assumes 12 hours a day every day is rarely followed.
  • Re-plan when things change. If you fall behind, change the start date to today and update your confidence ratings. The plan is rebuilt for the days that remain.

Assumptions and Limitations

  • A heuristic, not a guarantee. The method is a transparent rule of thumb. It cannot know your syllabus size, your learning speed or the weight of each paper, so use the optional “hours needed” field when you have a firm estimate.
  • Whole days. Hours are set per day type (weekday or weekend), not per date. A day with a holiday or a trip can be handled by adjusting the weekly rest day or the start date.
  • Exam day excluded. The plan assumes no studying for a subject on its own exam day. If two exams fall on the same day, both stop receiving time the day before.
  • Countdown uses your device clock. Each countdown is calculated in your device’s time zone from the exam start time you enter. Check the official timetable and report-in time from your institution.
  • Limits. Up to 12 subjects, exams within 366 days and up to 16 study hours a day.
  • Privacy. Your subjects and settings are stored only in this browser’s local storage and are never uploaded.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

How does the study hours allocator decide how much time each subject gets?
Each subject gets a priority weight equal to its difficulty (1 to 5) multiplied by 6 minus your confidence (1 to 5). Each day, that weight is multiplied by an urgency boost of 1 plus 7 divided by the days left until that exam, and your available hours are shared in proportion to the resulting scores. Harder subjects, weaker confidence and nearer exams therefore receive more time.
Why not simply split my study time equally between subjects?
An equal split gives the same time to a subject you already know well and one you find difficult, and it ignores which exam comes first. Weighting by difficulty, confidence and urgency moves hours to where they raise your marks the most. If you prefer an equal split, set the same difficulty and confidence for every subject and the same exam date.
What do the difficulty and confidence ratings mean?
Difficulty (1 easy to 5 very hard) is how demanding the subject is for you. Confidence (1 very weak to 5 strong) is how well you know the material right now. A hard subject you feel weak in scores highest and gets the most time. Re-rate your confidence every week or two so the plan follows your progress.
Does the plan include study time on the day of the exam?
No. A subject receives hours up to the day before its exam, and after that its time is shared among the remaining subjects. If you want to study on the morning of an exam, treat that as extra time on top of the plan.
What does the Short by or On track message mean?
If you enter the total hours you think a subject needs, the calculator compares it with the hours planned for that subject. On track means the plan covers your target and shows the spare time. Short by means the plan falls below your target by that amount. To fix a shortfall, add study hours, remove a rest day or lower the target.
How is the countdown calculated, and which time zone does it use?
The countdown subtracts the current time on your device from the exam date and start time you entered, and it updates every second. It uses your device's time zone, so if you travel or change your clock settings the countdown changes. Always confirm exam dates and times with your institution's official timetable.
Can I use this for competitive exams or university semester exams?
Yes. It works for any set of dated exams, including school boards, university semesters and competitive tests, with up to 12 subjects and exams up to a year away. For very large syllabi, split a subject into separate rows such as Physics Mechanics and Physics Optics, each with its own date and difficulty.
Is my study plan saved, and can I take it with me?
Your subjects and study-hour settings are saved automatically in your browser so they are there when you return on the same device. Nothing is sent to a server. To take the schedule elsewhere, use the Download schedule button in the day-by-day section to get a CSV file that opens in Excel, Google Sheets or similar apps.
