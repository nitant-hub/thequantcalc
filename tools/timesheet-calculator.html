What Is a Timesheet Calculator?

A timesheet records how long a person worked. A timesheet calculator turns raw start times, end times and breaks into totals you can use: net hours for each entry, a total for the day, week, fortnight or month, decimal hours for payroll and, optionally, an estimate of gross pay. Doing this by hand is error-prone because time is not decimal. Adding 8:45 and 7:30 digit by digit gives a meaningless 15:75, while the correct total is 16:15.

The calculator suits employees, freelancers, contractors, students on paid placements and managers who check hours before approving them. It uses plain arithmetic that applies anywhere in the world, with no country-specific payroll rules built in.

What This Calculator Works Out

  • Net time for every entry: end time minus start time minus any unpaid break.
  • The total for the chosen period in HH:MM and as decimal hours.
  • Days worked, the number of entries and the average length of a worked day.
  • An optional split between regular time and overtime.
  • An optional gross pay estimate from your hourly rate.

How to Use the Timesheet Calculator

  1. Pick a periodChoose Daily, Weekly, Biweekly or Monthly, then set the date. The date range it covers is shown under the field.
  2. Fill in your entriesEnter the work day, start time, end time and unpaid break in minutes. Leave unused entries empty.
  3. Add or remove entriesPress Add for another shift. Remove deletes the entry you last clicked in, or the last one, and always keeps at least one.
  4. Set optional pay detailsEnter an hourly rate, and if overtime applies, the hours limit, its basis and the multiplier.

Totals update as you type. “Copy first entry times to all” saves effort when shifts are identical. The time fields follow your browser and device settings, so they may show 12-hour or 24-hour format, but the calculation is the same either way.

Formulas Used

Net time for one entry

Net minutes = (End − Start) − Break

If the end is earlier than the start, 1,440 minutes are added to the difference because the shift finishes on the next day. The shift belongs to the date it started.

Period total and display formats

Total minutes = Net(1) + Net(2) + … + Net(n) Hours = floor(Total ÷ 60), Minutes = Total mod 60 Decimal hours = Total ÷ 60

Carrying minutes into hours is the normalisation step: 75 minutes becomes 1 hour 15 minutes.

Overtime and gross pay

Overtime = sum of max(0, Group total − Limit × 60) Gross = Rate × Regular ÷ 60 + Rate × Multiplier × Overtime ÷ 60

A group is one day, one 7-day block counted from the start date, or the whole period, depending on the basis you select. Without a limit, every minute is paid at the normal rate.

Overtime and Gross Pay Estimates

Overtime rules are set by law, contract or company policy and vary widely. Some systems count overtime per day, some per week, and some pay premiums on weekends or holidays. In the United States, for example, the Department of Labor publishes guidance on federal overtime, while other countries use different thresholds. Because this calculator is global, you supply the limit, basis and multiplier that apply to you.

Treat gross pay as an estimate. It excludes tax, social contributions, benefits, bonuses and allowances, so it is higher than take-home pay.

Assumptions and Limitations

  • Weekly blocks for overtime are 7 days counted from the period start, which for Monthly is the 1st of the month.
  • Times are clock times. Daylight-saving changes are not applied, so a shift across a clock change can differ from real elapsed time by an hour.
  • An overnight shift is counted on its start date, and overlapping shifts are rejected rather than merged.
  • Identical start and end times count as zero hours and trigger a warning, not 24 hours.
  • Up to 62 entries are supported. No rounding policy, paid break, holiday premium or deduction is applied.

Common Timesheet Mistakes

  • Entering a break in hours when the field expects minutes.
  • Forgetting to deduct an unpaid lunch break.
  • Reading 7:30 as 7.30 hours instead of 7.5.
  • Mixing up AM and PM on a 12-hour clock.
  • Treating gross pay as the amount that will be paid out.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the timesheet calculator work out hours?

For each entry it subtracts the start time from the end time, then subtracts the unpaid break. All arithmetic uses whole minutes, so every entry is exact, and the period total is the sum of the entries. Leave the break empty if your breaks are paid.

What do the Daily, Weekly, Biweekly and Monthly periods do?

The period sets the date range the entries must fall in. Daily covers one date, Weekly covers 7 days from the start date you choose, Biweekly covers 14 days from the start date, and Monthly covers the whole calendar month that contains the date you choose.

How do I enter a shift that finishes after midnight?

Enter the real start and end clock times, for example 22:00 and 06:00, on the date the shift began. When the end time is earlier than the start time, the calculator adds 24 hours to the difference. If the two times are identical, the entry counts as zero hours and a warning asks you to check them.

Can I enter more than one shift on the same day?

Yes. Choose Daily and press Add for each extra shift, or add more entries with the same date in any other period. Shifts that overlap are flagged as errors so the same minutes are never counted twice.

What is the difference between 7:30 and 7.5 hours?

They are the same amount of time written two ways. 7:30 means 7 hours and 30 minutes, while 7.5 is decimal hours, where 30 minutes is half an hour. Payroll systems usually multiply decimal hours by an hourly rate, so the calculator shows both formats.

How is overtime calculated?

Overtime is optional. Enter the number of hours after which overtime starts and choose whether that limit applies per day, per week (each 7-day block from the start date) or per whole period. Minutes above the limit are paid at your hourly rate times the overtime multiplier.

Does it follow my country's payroll rules, and is gross pay my take-home pay?

No to both. The calculator is global and uses plain arithmetic only, so check your own contract and local rules for thresholds, premiums, rounding and paid breaks. Gross pay is the amount before tax and other deductions, so it is higher than take-home pay.

Is my timesheet data saved or sent anywhere?

The calculations run in your browser with JavaScript, and no account is needed. The calculator does not save your entries, so refreshing the page clears them.

Conclusion

Accurate timesheets depend on correct time arithmetic. By converting every entry into whole minutes, subtracting breaks and summing the period, this calculator gives a reliable total in HH:MM and decimal form, plus an optional overtime split and gross pay estimate. Check the result against your own contract or local rules before using it for payroll or invoicing.
