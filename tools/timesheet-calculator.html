What Is a Timesheet Calculator?

A timesheet records how long a person worked on each day of a pay period. A timesheet calculator turns the raw start times, end times and breaks into totals you can use: net hours for each day, a weekly total, decimal hours for payroll and, optionally, an estimate of gross pay. Doing this by hand is error-prone because time is not decimal. An hour has 60 minutes, so adding 8:45 and 7:30 digit by digit produces a meaningless 15:75, while the correct total is 16:15.

This calculator is useful for employees, freelancers, contractors, students on paid placements and managers who check hours before approving them. It uses plain arithmetic that applies anywhere in the world, with no country-specific payroll rules built in.

What This Calculator Works Out

  • Net time for each day: the end time minus the start time minus any unpaid break.
  • A weekly total shown as hours and minutes (HH:MM) and as decimal hours.
  • The number of days worked and the average length of a worked day.
  • An optional split between regular time and overtime, based on a weekly threshold you choose.
  • An optional gross pay estimate based on your hourly rate.

How to Use the Timesheet Calculator

  1. Enter start and end timesFill in the start and end time for every day you worked and leave days off blank. For a night shift, enter the real clock times.
  2. Add unpaid breaksType each unpaid break in whole minutes. Leave the field empty if the break is paid.

The “Copy Monday to Tue–Fri” button saves time when your weekday shifts are identical. You can then adjust any single day.

Formulas Used

Daily net time

Net minutes = (End − Start) − Break

If the end time is earlier than the start time, 1,440 minutes are added to the difference because the shift finishes on the following day.

Weekly total and display formats

Total minutes = Net(Mon) + Net(Tue) + … + Net(Sun) Hours = floor(Total ÷ 60), Minutes = Total mod 60 Decimal hours = Total ÷ 60

Carrying minutes into hours in this way is the normalisation step: 75 minutes becomes 1 hour 15 minutes.

Overtime and gross pay

Regular minutes = min(Total, Threshold × 60) Overtime minutes = max(0, Total − Threshold × 60) Gross = Rate × Regular ÷ 60 + Rate × Multiplier × Overtime ÷ 60

Without a threshold, every minute is paid at the normal rate.

Reading Your Result: HH:MM or Decimal Hours

HH:MM is easiest to read, while decimal hours are what most payroll and invoicing systems multiply by a rate. Never type a time such as 7:30 as 7.30 in a rate calculation. Use the decimal figure shown in the result instead. The table lists common conversions.

Overtime and Gross Pay Estimates

Overtime rules are set by law, contract or company policy and vary widely. Some systems count overtime weekly, some daily, and some pay premium rates on weekends or public holidays. In the United States, for example, the Department of Labor publishes guidance on federal overtime requirements, while other countries use different thresholds and rates. Because this calculator is global, it asks you to supply the threshold and multiplier that apply to you instead of assuming them.

Treat the gross pay figure as an estimate. It excludes tax, social contributions, benefits, bonuses and allowances, so it will be higher than take-home pay.

Assumptions and Limitations

  • Each calculation covers one week with seven rows. For a longer period, calculate each week and add the totals.
  • Times are treated as clock times. Daylight-saving changes are not applied, so a shift that spans a clock change can differ from real elapsed time by an hour.
  • Each day holds one shift. For a split shift, enter the first start and the last end, then put the gap in the break field.
  • Identical start and end times count as zero hours, not 24.
  • No rounding policy, paid break, holiday premium or deduction is applied.

Common Timesheet Mistakes

  • Entering a break in hours when the field expects minutes.
  • Forgetting to deduct an unpaid lunch break.
  • Reading 7:30 as 7.30 hours instead of 7.5 hours.
  • Mixing up AM and PM when using a 12-hour clock.
  • Treating gross pay as the amount that will be paid out.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the timesheet calculator work out daily hours?

For each day it subtracts the start time from the end time, then subtracts the unpaid break. All arithmetic is done in whole minutes, so each daily result is exact, and the weekly total is the sum of the daily results.

How do I enter a shift that finishes after midnight?

Enter the real start and end clock times, for example 22:00 and 06:00. When the end time is earlier than the start time, the calculator treats the shift as ending the next day and adds 24 hours to the difference. If the start and end times are identical, that day counts as zero hours.

What is the difference between 7:30 and 7.5 hours?

They are the same amount of time written in two ways. 7:30 means 7 hours and 30 minutes, while 7.5 is decimal hours, where 30 minutes is half of an hour. Payroll systems usually multiply decimal hours by an hourly rate, so the calculator shows both formats.

Are breaks subtracted from my total?

Yes. Whatever you enter in the break field is treated as unpaid and is subtracted from that day. Leave the field blank or at zero if your breaks are paid.

How is overtime calculated?

Overtime is optional. If you enter a weekly threshold in hours, every minute above it is paid at your hourly rate multiplied by the overtime multiplier, and the minutes up to the threshold are paid at the normal rate. Leave the threshold blank to ignore overtime.

Does it follow my country's payroll or labour rules?

No. The calculator is global and applies plain arithmetic only. Overtime thresholds, daily limits, rounding policies, paid breaks and premium rates differ between countries, industries and contracts, so check your own rules before relying on the figures.

Is the gross pay estimate the same as take-home pay?

No. Gross pay is the amount earned before tax, social contributions and other deductions. The calculator does not estimate deductions, benefits, bonuses or allowances.

Is my timesheet data saved or sent anywhere?

The calculations run in your browser with JavaScript, and no account is needed. The calculator does not save your entries, so refreshing the page clears them.

Conclusion

Accurate timesheets depend on correct time arithmetic. By converting every shift into whole minutes, subtracting breaks and summing the week, this calculator gives you a reliable total in both HH:MM and decimal form, plus an optional overtime split and gross pay estimate. Check the result against your own contract or local rules before using it for payroll or invoicing.
