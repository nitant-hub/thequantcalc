CTC, Gross Salary and In-Hand Salary: Three Different Numbers

An offer letter quotes your Cost to Company (CTC), but the amount that reaches your bank account each month is smaller, sometimes by a surprising margin. CTC is what the employer spends on you in a year: your cash salary, plus the employer's own Provident Fund (PF) contribution, a gratuity provision, group insurance and any other benefits. Your gross salary is only the cash part of that. Your in-hand (take-home) salary is what remains once the employee PF, professional tax, income tax deducted at source (TDS) and any other payroll deductions are taken out of the gross.

The In-Hand Salary Calculator on this page rebuilds that chain step by step for the Indian financial year 2026-27 (assessment year 2027-28). It shows the monthly and annual figure, the split of your CTC, and the full tax working, so you can see exactly where each rupee goes instead of trusting a single unexplained number.

How the Calculator Gets From CTC to In-Hand Pay

The calculation runs in five stages. Every figure is computed with full precision and rounded to the nearest rupee only when displayed.

  1. Split the CTC. Variable pay is separated out. The remaining fixed CTC is divided into basic salary (your chosen percentage), HRA (a percentage of basic) and a special allowance that absorbs whatever is left.
  2. Remove employer-side items. Employer PF, the gratuity provision, insurance and employer NPS are paid on your behalf and are not part of your cash salary, so they are subtracted from CTC to reach gross salary.
  3. Apply deductions allowed for your regime. The new regime allows the standard deduction and employer NPS. The old regime also allows HRA exemption, professional tax, Section 80C, 80D, NPS 80CCD(1B), home-loan interest and others.
  4. Compute income tax. Slab tax, then the Section 87A rebate (with marginal relief), surcharge where applicable, and 4% health and education cess.
  5. Subtract payroll deductions. Employee PF, professional tax, income tax and other monthly deductions come off the gross to give the net figure, which is divided by 12.
Basic = Fixed CTC × Basic%
Employer PF = 12% × min(Basic/12, PF wage ceiling) × 12
Gratuity = 4.81% × Basic
Gross salary = CTC − Employer PF − Gratuity − Insurance − Employer NPS
Taxable income = Gross + Employer NPS − Standard deduction − other allowed deductions
In-hand (year) = Gross − Employee PF − Professional tax − Income tax − Other deductions

Why Basic Salary Drives Almost Everything

Basic salary is the base on which PF, gratuity and the HRA exemption are all calculated. A higher basic raises your PF and gratuity (long-term savings) and lowers your take-home today. A lower basic does the opposite. That is why two offers with the same CTC can have different monthly in-hand pay. This calculator lets you change the basic percentage to see the trade-off directly.

PF, Gratuity and the Recent Rule Changes

Three recent developments matter for take-home pay, and many online calculators have not caught up with them:

  • New labour codes. Provisions of the Code on Wages came into force on 21 November 2025. Under the unified definition of “wages” (basic pay, dearness allowance and retaining allowance), wages are expected to make up at least 50% of total remuneration. Employers have been restructuring pay so that basic is a larger share, which raises PF and gratuity and can lower take-home. The calculator therefore defaults to a 50% basic. See the Ministry of Labour & Employment for official notifications.
  • Higher EPF wage ceiling. Reported notification S.O. 5109(E), dated 17 September 2026, raises the statutory EPF wage ceiling from ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 per month with prospective effect. At 12%, the PF on the ceiling rises from ₹1,800 to ₹3,000 per month for each of employee and employer. The calculator defaults to ₹25,000 and offers the old ₹15,000 ceiling and “full basic” options. Check the latest circular on EPFO’s website and your payslip, because payroll systems adopt changes at different speeds.
  • Income-tax Act, 2025. From 1 April 2026 the Income-tax Act, 1961 is replaced by the Income-tax Act, 2025, with renumbered sections (the new-regime provision formerly in section 115BAC is now section 202). Budget 2026 left the slab rates, rebate and standard deduction unchanged. This page uses the familiar names (80C, 80D, 80CCD) because most payslips and Form 12BB still do.

Gratuity Is Not Cash in Hand

Gratuity is commonly provisioned at 15 days of basic pay per year of service, which works out to 15 ÷ (26 × 12) = 4.81% of annual basic. It is counted in CTC but you receive it only when you leave after qualifying service (generally five years of continuous service; the new codes shorten this for fixed-term employees). It never appears in your monthly salary credit.

New Regime or Old Regime?

The new regime has lower slab rates but removes most deductions. The old regime keeps HRA, 80C, 80D, home-loan interest and similar claims, so it can win when those claims are large. The calculator always computes both regimes for the same salary and tells you which one leaves more money, so you do not have to guess. Example 2 below shows that even ₹4.98 lakh of old-regime deductions did not beat the new regime at a ₹25 lakh CTC.

Tip: Employed taxpayers can switch regimes each year when filing the return, but your employer needs your choice early in the year to deduct the right TDS. If you choose later, any difference is settled when you file.

Step-by-Step Worked Examples

Example 1: ₹12,00,000 CTC, Default Structure

Assume basic is 50% of CTC, HRA is 50% of basic, PF is on the ₹25,000 ceiling, gratuity is inside CTC and professional tax is ₹2,400 a year.

  1. Salary split: Basic = ₹6,00,000; HRA = ₹3,00,000.
  2. Employer-side items: Employer PF = 12% × ₹25,000 × 12 = ₹36,000; gratuity = 4.81% × ₹6,00,000 = ₹28,860.
  3. Special allowance and gross: Special = 12,00,000 − 36,000 − 28,860 − 6,00,000 − 3,00,000 = ₹2,35,140. Gross salary = ₹11,35,140.
  4. New regime tax: Taxable income = 11,35,140 − 75,000 = ₹10,60,140. Slab tax = 5% × 4,00,000 + 10% × 2,60,140 = ₹46,014, which the Section 87A rebate cancels completely. Tax = ₹0.
  5. In-hand: 11,35,140 − 36,000 (employee PF) − 2,400 (professional tax) = ₹10,96,740 a year = ₹91,395 per month, which is 91.4% of CTC.

Under the old regime with no investments or rent, taxable income is ₹10,46,740, tax with cess is ₹1,31,583 and in-hand falls to ₹80,430 per month. The new regime is clearly better in this case.

Example 2: ₹25,00,000 CTC, Metro City, Heavy Old-Regime Claims

Same structure, with rent of ₹30,000 a month in a metro, ₹1,00,000 of 80C investments besides EPF, ₹25,000 of health insurance and ₹50,000 of own NPS.

  1. Split: Basic = ₹12,50,000; HRA = ₹6,25,000; employer PF = ₹36,000; gratuity = ₹60,125; gross salary = ₹24,03,875.
  2. New regime: Taxable = 24,03,875 − 75,000 = ₹23,28,875. Slab tax = 20,000 + 40,000 + 60,000 + 80,000 + 25% × 3,28,875 = ₹2,82,219; with 4% cess = ₹2,93,508. In-hand = ₹20,71,968 a year = ₹1,72,664 per month.
  3. HRA exemption (old regime): the least of ₹6,25,000 (HRA received), ₹3,60,000 − ₹1,25,000 = ₹2,35,000 (rent minus 10% of basic) and ₹6,25,000 (50% of basic) = ₹2,35,000.
  4. Old regime deductions: 50,000 (standard) + 2,35,000 (HRA) + 2,400 (professional tax) + 1,36,000 (80C: ₹36,000 EPF + ₹1,00,000) + 50,000 (NPS) + 25,000 (80D) = ₹4,98,400. Taxable = 24,03,875 − 4,98,400 = ₹19,05,475.
  5. Old regime tax: 12,500 + 1,00,000 + 30% × 9,05,475 = ₹3,84,143; with cess = ₹3,99,508. In-hand = ₹1,63,831 per month.

The new regime leaves about ₹8,833 more every month (₹1,06,000 less tax a year) even though this taxpayer claimed nearly ₹5 lakh of deductions.

Assumptions, Limitations and Practical Tips

  • Estimate, not a payslip. Employers calculate TDS from a projected annual income and revise it during the year. Your actual monthly TDS can differ, especially when variable pay is paid in one lump.
  • Variable pay is averaged. Tax is worked out on the full-year total, and the net result is spread over 12 months. In the month a bonus is paid, your take-home will be higher.
  • Salary income only. Interest, rental income, capital gains, a previous employer's salary in the same year and other income are not included.
  • Age and residency. The slabs are for a resident individual below 60. Senior citizens, non-residents and HUFs are taxed differently.
  • Employer contributions above ₹7.5 lakh. The calculator taxes the portion of employer PF and NPS above ₹7,50,000 a year, which only matters for very high CTCs.
  • Professional tax varies by state. Some states charge nothing. Enter the amount from your payslip.
  • Payroll practice varies. Some employers pay PF on full basic, include a lunch or fuel allowance, or structure gratuity differently. Use the “Statutory ceiling” and “Full basic” options and the benefit fields to match your offer letter.
  • Not tax advice. For filing decisions, confirm with a chartered accountant or the official portal.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What is the difference between CTC, gross salary and in-hand salary?
CTC (Cost to Company) is the total yearly amount your employer spends on you, including employer PF, a gratuity provision, insurance and other benefits. Gross salary is the cash part of CTC before any deductions. In-hand salary is what reaches your bank account after employee PF, professional tax, income tax (TDS) and other payroll deductions are subtracted from gross salary. In-hand pay is therefore always lower than CTC.
How much of my CTC will I actually receive each month?
It depends on your salary structure and tax regime. With a basic salary of 50% of CTC, PF on the 25,000 rupee ceiling and the new tax regime, a 12,00,000 rupee CTC gives about 10,96,740 rupees a year in hand (91.4% of CTC, or about 91,395 rupees a month) because the Section 87A rebate removes the income tax. At a 25,00,000 rupee CTC the same structure gives about 20,71,968 rupees a year (82.9%, or about 1,72,664 rupees a month) because tax becomes significant.
Is income up to 12 lakh rupees really tax-free in the new regime?
Taxable income up to 12,00,000 rupees gets a Section 87A rebate of up to 60,000 rupees in the new regime, so no income tax is payable on it. Salaried taxpayers also get a 75,000 rupee standard deduction, so salary up to 12,75,000 rupees can be tax-free. The rebate does not apply to income taxed at special rates, such as certain capital gains. Above the limit, marginal relief makes sure the tax does not exceed the income earned over 12,00,000 rupees.
Should I choose the old or the new tax regime?
Compare both on your own numbers. The new regime has lower slab rates but allows only the standard deduction and employer NPS, while the old regime allows HRA, Section 80C, 80D, home-loan interest and similar deductions. The old regime tends to win only when your total claims are large. This calculator computes both regimes for the same salary and shows the yearly tax difference and monthly in-hand pay under each.
How does the higher EPF wage ceiling change my take-home pay?
A reported notification dated 17 September 2026 raised the EPF statutory wage ceiling from 15,000 rupees to 25,000 rupees per month. At 12%, PF on the ceiling rises from 1,800 rupees to 3,000 rupees a month for both employee and employer, so the employee deduction can reduce monthly take-home by up to 1,200 rupees. The change is prospective, and payroll systems adopt it at different times, so check your payslip and select the matching ceiling option in the calculator.
Does the 50% wages rule under the new labour codes reduce my in-hand salary?
It can. Under the unified definition of wages, basic pay plus dearness allowance is expected to be at least 50% of total remuneration. A higher basic raises PF and gratuity, which are long-term benefits, and lowers cash take-home for the same CTC. If your employer restructured your pay after the codes took effect on 21 November 2025, set the Basic percentage in the calculator to match your new salary structure.
Is gratuity part of my monthly in-hand salary?
No. Gratuity is included in CTC as a provision of about 4.81% of basic salary, which is 15 days of basic pay for each year of service. It is paid as a lump sum when you leave the organisation after qualifying service, generally five years of continuous service. It is not credited to your bank account each month, so it lowers in-hand pay relative to CTC.
Why is my actual payslip different from this estimate?
Employers deduct TDS from a projected annual income and may revise it during the year. Differences also come from the timing of variable pay, income from a previous employer, perquisites, the tax regime you declared, investment proofs submitted late, state-specific professional tax and company-specific allowances. Treat the calculator as a planning estimate and use your payslip as the final figure.
