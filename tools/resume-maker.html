      Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS): What Actually Matters

      Many employers use an applicant tracking system to collect and search resumes. Parsers differ in quality, so the safest approach is to make your resume easy to read in plain text. In general:

      • Prefer a single column. Older parsers can read two-column layouts in the wrong order, mixing your skills into your job history. The styles tagged ATS-safe use one column.
      • Use standard section names such as Work Experience, Education and Skills. This tool does so for every layout.
      • Keep text as text. Information inside images, including a photo, cannot be read. Skill “bars” and rating dots convey nothing to a parser, so this tool does not offer them.
      • Use the plain-text copy when an application form asks you to paste your resume. The tool produces a clean version with simple dashes for bullets.
      • Match the job description honestly. Use the same terms the advertisement uses for skills you genuinely have.

      The health check beside the editor warns you when your chosen layout uses columns or includes a photo, so you can decide knowingly instead of guessing.

      Writing Bullet Points That Get Read

      Style gets a resume opened; content gets it shortlisted. A reliable pattern for each bullet is action verb + what you did + measurable result. Numbers (time saved, money, users, accuracy) make the claim concrete, and if you do not have exact figures, an honest approximation such as “about 30 clients” is better than a vague statement.

      Bullet = Action verb + Task or scope + Result (number, %, time, money)

      Example 1: Turning a Duty Into an Achievement

      1. Before: “Responsible for handling customer support tickets.” This states a duty, not a result, and many candidates could write it.
      2. Add scope: “Handled 60+ customer support tickets daily across email and chat.”
      3. Add outcome: “Resolved 60+ tickets daily across email and chat while raising the satisfaction score from 82% to 91%.”

      The final line starts with a strong verb, shows volume, and proves quality. Use only figures you can defend in an interview.

      Example 2: A Fresher Resume With No Work Experience

      1. Reorder sections. Open Section order & visibility on the Content tab and move Education above Experience, with Skills and Projects directly after it. Untick Experience if you have none.
      2. Use projects as evidence. Describe one or two academic or personal projects with the tools used and an outcome, such as “Built a budgeting web app used by 25 classmates; reduced data entry time by half with CSV import.”
      3. Pick a safe style. Choose the Corporate or Minimal recipe (both tagged ATS-safe), keep it to one page and check that the health check shows no warnings.

      Assumptions, Limitations and Practical Tips

      • Fonts are system fonts. To keep the page fast and private, the tool uses fonts already installed on your device. A font such as Calibri or Garamond falls back to a similar font where it is missing, so the PDF you save on one computer may differ slightly from a PDF saved on another. Georgia, Arial and Times are the most consistent.
      • PDF export uses your browser’s print dialog. Choose “Save as PDF”, set margins to “Default” and turn off headers and footers. Enable background graphics if your browser shows that option, so banner and sidebar colours print.
      • Banner and sidebar layouts are designed for one page. They run to the page edge, so a second page has no top margin. If your content is longer, use a Classic or Modern layout, which keeps your chosen margins on every page.
      • The red dashed lines in the preview show where a new page starts. They do not appear in the PDF.
      • No spell check or grammar check. Proofread twice and ask someone else to read it. Your browser’s spell check underlines errors in the text boxes.
      • Region and norms vary. Photos, date of birth and personal details are customary in some countries and discouraged in others. Follow the norms of the market you are applying in.
      • No guarantee of results. A well-designed resume improves readability but cannot replace relevant experience or a good fit with the role.
